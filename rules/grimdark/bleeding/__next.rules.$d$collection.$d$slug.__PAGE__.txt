3:T773,<p><strong>Added:</strong> a cumulative wound condition.</p>
<p>Bleeding is an explicit exception to the normal 2024 rule that a condition does not stack with itself. Each time a creature gains Bleeding, add the new level to its current Bleeding level.</p>
<p>At the start of each of a bleeding creature's turns, follow this sequence:</p>
<ol>
<li>Roll one of the creature's Hit Dice—not its remaining or total Hit Dice—and lose that many Hit Points. The die explodes recursively like every other Grimdark damage die.</li>
<li>Add 1 to the Hit Points lost for each Bleeding level beyond the first. For example, Bleeding 3 on a creature with a d10 Hit Die causes <code>1d10 + 2</code> Hit Point loss.</li>
<li>After losing Hit Points, if the creature is at 0 Hit Points, it gains the Unconscious condition.</li>
<li>If the creature is both bleeding and Unconscious, roll <code>1d20 - 1</code> for each Bleeding level beyond the first. On a modified result of 1 or lower, the creature bleeds out and dies unless Fate or another appropriate effect prevents that death.</li>
</ol>
<p>Bleeding Hit Point loss bypasses Damage Negation. It does not produce Excess Damage or another Critical Injury; the Unconscious and bleed-out procedure replaces those consequences for Bleeding damage.</p>
<p>Each Bleeding level that ends is replaced by 1 Exhaustion level. Ending Bleeding 3 therefore leaves the creature with Exhaustion 3. This conversion can raise a creature to Exhaustion 6, which is fatal under the 2024 Exhaustion condition.</p>
<p>When a creature has more than one Hit Die type, use its largest Hit Die for now. This is the current Foundry automation default and remains subject to playtesting. Successful treatment of a Critical Injury removes the Bleeding caused by that injury. A general First Aid action and the interaction between Bleeding and ordinary magical Hit Point healing remain open.</p>

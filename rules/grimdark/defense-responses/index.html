Changes these 2024 rules:Combat

When a melee attack is declared, use the response that best describes how the defender meets it.

Parry

Opposed; risk and reward. Roll d20 + proficiency bonus + Strength or Dexterity modifier. A successful Parry wins the exchange, prevents normal weapon damage, and gains Momentum. On a natural 20, the defender inflicts a Critical Injury on the attacker even if the Parry loses.

Dodge

Opposed; safety. Roll d20 + proficiency bonus + Dexterity modifier. A successful Dodge wins the exchange, prevents normal weapon damage, and gains Momentum. On a natural 20, the defender negates all damage even if the Dodge loses. An attacker who still wins the opposed exchange gains Momentum.

If the attack and Dodge both roll a natural 20, the default rule gives the Dodge priority: the attack deals no damage and inflicts no attacker Critical Injury. The GM can instead configure the world to let the Dodge negate normal damage while the attacker still inflicts a Critical Injury, or to cancel both critical effects and resolve the exchange normally from the roll totals.

Tank the Hit

Voluntary and unopposed. Make no defense roll. Add the defender's Strength modifier to Damage Negation for that hit. Tanking cannot win the exchange, generate Momentum, or trigger a defensive critical.

Unopposed

The defender makes no defense roll, usually because they cannot respond. The attack hits automatically unless its attack die is a natural 1.

Surprised

The defender makes no defense roll. Under the 2024 baseline, being surprised normally gives Disadvantage on Initiative; this Grimdark response adds further danger when an attack catches a target unable to respond.

When a Surprised attack successfully deals damage, it automatically inflicts one unmodified Critical Injury in addition to its normal damage. Roll damage normally, including exploding dice; do not double the number of damage dice. Surprise itself adds no modifier to the Critical Injury severity roll, though modifiers from other rules still apply.

If the same attack also triggers a Critical Injury through a natural 20 or Excess Damage, resolve only one Critical Injury. Use the Excess Damage modifier when applicable.

The 2014 Assassin's automatic-critical-hit interaction with Surprise is legacy material and does not create a second Critical Injury. Grimdark 5e uses the revised 2024 rules as its primary baseline; a dedicated 2014 subclass conversion can replace that redundant legacy clause.

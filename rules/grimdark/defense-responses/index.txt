10:Ta63,<p>When a melee attack is declared, use the response that best describes how the defender meets it.</p>
<h3>Parry</h3>
<p><strong>Opposed; risk and reward.</strong> Roll <code>d20 + proficiency bonus + Strength or Dexterity modifier</code>. A successful Parry wins the exchange, prevents normal weapon damage, and gains Momentum. On a natural 20, the defender inflicts a Critical Injury on the attacker even if the Parry loses.</p>
<h3>Dodge</h3>
<p><strong>Opposed; safety.</strong> Roll <code>d20 + proficiency bonus + Dexterity modifier</code>. A successful Dodge wins the exchange, prevents normal weapon damage, and gains Momentum. On a natural 20, the defender negates all damage even if the Dodge loses. An attacker who still wins the opposed exchange gains Momentum.</p>
<p>If the attack and Dodge both roll a natural 20, the default rule gives the Dodge priority: the attack deals no damage and inflicts no attacker Critical Injury. The GM can instead configure the world to let the Dodge negate normal damage while the attacker still inflicts a Critical Injury, or to cancel both critical effects and resolve the exchange normally from the roll totals.</p>
<h3>Tank the Hit</h3>
<p><strong>Voluntary and unopposed.</strong> Make no defense roll. Add the defender's Strength modifier to Damage Negation for that hit. Tanking cannot win the exchange, generate Momentum, or trigger a defensive critical.</p>
<h3>Unopposed</h3>
<p>The defender makes no defense roll, usually because they cannot respond. The attack hits automatically unless its attack die is a natural 1.</p>
<h3>Surprised</h3>
<p>The defender makes no defense roll. Under the 2024 baseline, being surprised normally gives Disadvantage on Initiative; this Grimdark response adds further danger when an attack catches a target unable to respond.</p>
<p>When a Surprised attack successfully deals damage, it automatically inflicts one <strong>unmodified Critical Injury</strong> in addition to its normal damage. Roll damage normally, including exploding dice; do not double the number of damage dice. Surprise itself adds no modifier to the Critical Injury severity roll, though modifiers from other rules still apply.</p>
<p>If the same attack also triggers a Critical Injury through a natural 20 or Excess Damage, resolve only one Critical Injury. Use the Excess Damage modifier when applicable.</p>
<p>The 2014 Assassin's automatic-critical-hit interaction with Surprise is legacy material and does not create a second Critical Injury. Grimdark 5e uses the revised 2024 rules as its primary baseline; a dedicated 2014 subclass conversion can replace that redundant legacy clause.</p>

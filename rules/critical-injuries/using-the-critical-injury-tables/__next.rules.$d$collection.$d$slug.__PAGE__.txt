3:Ta81,<p><strong>Added:</strong> damage-type consequences for Critical Injuries.</p>
<p>When a creature suffers a Critical Injury, roll <code>1d100 + Excess Damage + 10 per existing Critical Injury</code> and consult the table matching the injury's damage type. Modified totals above 100 are intentional: Excess Damage and unrecovered injuries make mortal and fatal outcomes increasingly likely.</p>
<h3>Choose One Damage Type</h3>
<p>Use the only damage type dealt by the triggering attack or effect. If it dealt more than one type, use the type that contributed the most damage before Damage Negation. On a tie, the attacker chooses. For a Parry critical, use the defender's weapon damage type; an unarmed Parry normally uses Bludgeoning. A creature immune to the chosen type cannot suffer a Critical Injury of that type.</p>
<h3>No Separate Hit-Location Roll</h3>
<p>These tables already mix wounds to the head, torso, and limbs. Do not roll hit location. When a result names a plausible limb, organ, or sense, follow the fiction of the attack. If the fiction does not decide it, the injured creature chooses among equally plausible outcomes.</p>
<h3>Bleeding</h3>
<p><strong>Bleeding X</strong> is cumulative. At the end of each of your turns, lose Hit Points equal to your total Bleeding; this loss ignores Damage Negation. At 0 Hit Points, the loss becomes Excess Damage, but all Bleeding at the end of one turn can trigger no more than one additional Critical Injury.</p>
<p>As an action, you or an adjacent creature can attempt a Wisdom (Medicine) check with a DC equal to <code>10 + your total Bleeding</code>. On a success, reduce Bleeding by 1. A healing spell that restores Hit Points also reduces Bleeding by 1, but it does not remove the underlying Critical Injury unless it meets that injury's magical-healing threshold.</p>
<h3>Treatment and Recovery</h3>
<p>An injury's treatment check requires appropriate supplies and 10 minutes of work unless its effect sets a more urgent deadline. When an effect gives a deadline measured in turns, attempting that treatment is an action. Successful treatment removes Bleeding caused by that injury and begins its natural recovery. At the end of the listed number of completed long rests, the injury and its temporary effects end. Failed treatment can be attempted again after a short or long rest.</p>
<p>Magic cast with at least the listed spell-slot level can repair the injury if the spell is appropriate to the harm. Result-specific recovery text takes precedence; ordinary healing does not regrow missing anatomy, replace an erased mind, or restore life. All DCs, recovery times, and magical thresholds are first-playtest values.</p>
